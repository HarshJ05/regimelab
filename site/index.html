RegimeLab

Markets switch between calm periods and stressed ones. A Hidden Markov Model, written from scratch, works out which regime each futures market is probably in, using only data available at the time, and a simple rule steps out of the market when stress is likely. It is tested against buying and holding, a plain volatility rule, and a version that cheats by peeking at the future.

How it works

  1. Model. Each day the market is in one of K hidden regimes. Regimes follow a Markov chain (today's regime depends only on yesterday's), and daily returns in regime k are normal with their own mean and volatility.
  2. Fit. Baum-Welch (the EM algorithm for HMMs), implemented from scratch with several starting points. BIC chooses between 2 and 3 regimes. Regimes are named by volatility, because EM returns them in random order.
  3. Walk forward. Every 6 months the model is refitted on all data so far (at least 3 years), then the forward filter gives P(regime | returns up to today) for the next 6 months. No future data is used.
  4. Trade. At today's close: stay long if P(stressed) < 0.5, otherwise go flat. Applied to tomorrow's return, with 2 bp costs per switch.
  5. Keep it honest. Compare with buy & hold, with a simple rule (flat when 20-day volatility is above its 1-year median), and with a cheating version that uses smoothed, full-hindsight probabilities.

Limits. Yahoo Finance continuous front-month futures (roll jumps not adjusted); daily closes; one position size, no leverage; WTI's negative price on 20 April 2020 is removed. Research, not trading advice.
